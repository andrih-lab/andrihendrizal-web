Long-term ecological data is rare in Indonesia's mangrove forests, where most surveys are one-off snapshots. Three sites in Riau — Pangkalan Jambi and Bukit Batu in Bengkalis Regency, and Kelurahan Purnama in Dumai City — are tracked as permanent monitoring sites rather than visited just once.

Repeated visits to the same plots make it possible to see change that a single survey cannot: how mangrove density responds to marine debris accumulation in Purnama, or how local ecological knowledge at Pangkalan Jambi shifts as the forest itself changes. Both the forest plots and the surrounding communities are revisited on a regular cycle, and it is that resulting time series — not a one-time impression — that eventually supports evidence-based management recommendations.
