Fieldwork in mangrove forests means working from stilt roots as often as solid ground. Structural surveys — diameter at breast height, canopy height, stem density — still have to be measured by hand, tree by tree, even when a drone survey covers the wider stand.

The two methods complement each other. Ground measurements calibrate what a drone-based canopy model actually means in terms of biomass, while the drone imagery extends coverage far beyond what a field team can walk in a day. Combining a QGIS-based spatial workflow with R for the statistical side is what turns a set of field notebooks into a defensible density or carbon estimate.

None of this is unique to one site — the same protocol travels with the research team from the permanent monitoring sites in Bengkalis and Dumai to shorter engagements elsewhere in Riau.
